How it’s calculated

One multiplication

Every entry in CarbonLog is an activity times an emission factor. Here is the example from the home page.

2,400 kWhActivity
×
0.38kg CO₂ per kWh
=
912 kg CO₂Emissions

Activity-based

You measure what happened: kWh, kilometres or litres. This is the more accurate way, and CarbonLog uses it for energy and travel.

Spend-based

You use what you paid in euros and an average factor per euro. It is quick but rough, so CarbonLog uses it only for purchases.

Where to find data

You already have most of it

Energy bills

Electricity and gas bills list your consumption in kWh for each billing period.

→ Electricity, Natural gas

Mileage and fuel records

Logbooks, fuel cards or expense claims show how far company cars travelled.

→ Car travel

Travel bookings

Train and flight bookings show the route; look up the distance in kilometres.

→ Train travel, Flights

Invoices and accounting

Your bookkeeping shows what you spent on goods and services each month.

→ Purchased goods

Quick wins

Six first steps that rarely cost much

Switch your tariff

Ask your supplier for a certified green electricity tariff. It is often the biggest single change for an office.

Light with LED

Replace old bulbs and tubes, and add timers or motion sensors in rooms that are often empty.

Heat smarter

Lower the setpoint a little and add night and weekend schedules.

Take the train

For trips within the country or to neighbouring cities, the train is usually the low-carbon choice.

Meet online

Turn routine meetings into video calls and bundle the rest into fewer trips.

Buy less, buy better

Choose used or refurbished equipment and ask suppliers about their own emissions.

Glossary

Words you will meet

CO₂e
Carbon dioxide equivalent. Other greenhouse gases, such as methane, are converted into the amount of CO₂ with the same warming effect, so everything can be added up.
Emission factor
How much greenhouse gas one unit of an activity causes, for example kg CO₂ per kWh of electricity.
Baseline year
The year you compare against when you set a target. CarbonLog uses the previous calendar year.
Net zero
Cutting emissions as far as possible and balancing only the small remainder with permanent removals.
Offsetting
Paying for emission cuts or removals somewhere else. It does not reduce your own emissions, so treat it as a last step.
Carbon neutral
A claim that emissions are balanced, often with offsets. Claims like this are increasingly restricted in the EU, so be careful how you use them.
ESG
Environmental, Social and Governance: the three areas that investors, banks and customers look at.
GHG Protocol
The most widely used standard for measuring company greenhouse gas emissions. It defines Scopes 1, 2 and 3.
CSRD
The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. It sets reporting rules mainly for larger companies. Its thresholds and timeline have changed, so check the current rules for your company.
VSME
A voluntary sustainability reporting standard for small and medium-sized companies, published by EFRAG. It helps answer data requests from banks and larger customers.
